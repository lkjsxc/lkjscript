## 3. Chosen completion contract

### Read-only consumption is not publication permission

Preserve three separate conclusions: authentic candidate acceptance, the current publication-admission result, and successful installed consumption. An authentic candidate may be usable while publication is correctly refused. A successful `consume` grants no publication authority.

The whole-operation success rule is:

| Selected producer / transfer and installed smoke | Authority observation | Successful `consume`? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All required stages pass, including joined cleanup | Completed authorization | Yes; record authorized, do not publish |
| All required stages pass, including joined cleanup | Genuine completed rejection | Yes; record rejected and its reason, do not publish |
| Otherwise successful | Unavailable, cancelled, incomplete, missing, malformed, unknown or contradictory result | **No** |
| Any required producer, transfer, smoke or cleanup stage fails/is missing | Any | **No** |

A rejection is a fail-closed admission decision under the existing trusted-controller contract, not permission to relabel an unavailable API response or cancellation as a negative test. For this campaign's hosted witness, the required rejection is specifically the real annotated tag's different product source. A generic refusal for an unrelated reason does not prove that assertion.

Fix **both** the authority-step handling and terminal success predicate. Use the existing controller output/state contract. A successful process must agree with its expected successful state; a failed process is tolerable for `consume` only when it supplies a valid rejection from that authority operation. Missing or inconsistent diagnostic output fails closed. Do not introduce broad `continue-on-error`, `|| true`, fallback authorization, or a new spelling that turns uncertainty into rejection.

Keep incomplete/cancelled/unavailable diagnostics visible. A terminal may retain the existing `incomplete` status with its actual authority/job outcome rather than inventing a new schema. On real cancellation, preserve available evidence and do not claim a terminal upload occurred when CI prevented it. Terminal success must independently exclude non-determinate authority even when a test supplies `CONTROLLER=success`.

Preserve the stricter authorized-publication requirements of `promote` and `resume-publication`; preserve `resume-public`'s separate read-only semantics. Consume must continue to exclude publication/public jobs and perform no remote writes. A future successful rejection must not be mistaken for a successful release.
