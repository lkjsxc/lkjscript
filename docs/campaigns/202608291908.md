## 3. Objective and campaign selection

### Primary objective

Make the root `lkjscript` SemVer the only version presented by the current product-facing surface,
while preserving independently owned internal compatibility discriminants at their canonical
owners.

Complete one direct cutover across the distributed CLI, discovery, review projections,
operator-editable descriptors, resident events, generated public documentation, current prose,
release metadata, tests, and every maintained consumer. Delete the predecessor numbered public
surface rather than retaining aliases, fallback readers, or parallel documentation.

### Why this is the primary problem now

The higher-order authoring campaign is closed and no external recovery is active. The user's
explicit product policy now conflicts with executable behavior and current documentation. Leaving
the conflict in place would:

- continue teaching users and agents that internal generation numbers are product concepts;
- encourage unrelated internal contract owners to synchronize or leak through future features;
- make the public surface appear fragmented despite one distributed product;
- preserve stale duplicate authority between generated contract catalogs and actual internal
  owners; and
- make every later public feature repeat the same leakage.

After this campaign, internal formats remain free to evolve independently while the product surface
becomes smaller, more stable, and easier for both humans and agents to discover.

### Candidate comparison

1. **Selected: product-facing version-surface cutover.** Directly closes the explicit user policy,
   removes duplicate public authority, has clear maintained consumers and independent black-box
   proof, and can reach one coherent final state.
2. **Documentation-only terminology edit.** Smaller, but rejected because the executable,
   descriptors, runtime events, review files, generated documents, and release metadata would remain
   contradictory.
3. **Synchronize every internal contract with the product version.** Rejected because it couples
   unrelated compatibility domains, creates meaningless bumps, weakens format ownership, and
   contradicts the accepted release/version design.
4. **Delete internal format discriminants.** Rejected because strict decoding, stale-input
   rejection, cache validity, release verification, and corruption detection still need canonical
   internal owners.
5. **First-party semantic data engine and SQL/PostgreSQL cutover.** High long-term value but much
   larger, more empirical, and unrelated to the immediate public contradiction.
6. **Bounded context traversal.** A credible smaller feature, but it would add new public surface
   before the product identity boundary is corrected.
7. **Publish `v0.1.10`.** Publication is irreversible, is not authorized here, and should follow
   rather than precede this source cutover.

### Prioritized deferred candidates

1. first-party semantic data operations and complete maintained-consumer storage cutover;
2. bounded context traversal with revision-bound bounded continuation evidence;
3. the smallest remaining maintained authored-operation slice;
4. a separately authorized `v0.1.10` release campaign after this source state is complete and
   release-ready.

Do not implement these unless omission makes the selected objective impossible.

## 6. Mandates, selected design, latitude, and non-goals

### Mandates

- The only product-facing version is the root `lkjscript` product version.
- Internal compatibility domains remain independently owned; do not synchronize them.
- Public behavior retains complete deterministic discovery, diagnostics, bounds, and exact integrity
  binding.
- The cutover is direct. No compatibility aliases, dual output, fallback parser for current input,
  “legacy” flag, generation selector, or deprecated numbered public mode.
- Every maintained consumer migrates in this campaign.
- Historical records and immutable releases remain intact.
- Failed or predecessor input performs no semantic, filesystem-visible, runtime, secret, network, or
  external publication effect.
- No release or deployment is authorized.

### Selected design

Use a projection boundary rather than an internal renumbering campaign:

```text
root Cargo product version
            │
            ├── exact `lkjscript --version`
            └── product record in public capabilities / public metadata

typed internal compatibility owners
            │
            ├── storage, cache, artifact, plan-token, descriptor, runtime,
            │   adapter, and release-verifier binding
            └── opaque capabilities digest
                         │
                         ▼
stable unnumbered CLI grammar, review files, descriptors,
events, generated guides, current docs, and release metadata
```

The public projection may expose:

- the product name and product SemVer;
- stable operation/form/field names;
- exact semantic revisions, package revisions, artifact/content identities, and source/target
  identities where users need them;
- opaque integrity/capabilities digests;
- limits, effects, diagnostics, security nonclaims, and resource classifications.

It must not expose internal subsystem values as versions or generation brands.

The public capability digest binds the complete current executable projection. Internal contract
descriptors continue to bind internal encodings and behavior. Do not make the public digest semantic
authority or a handwritten constant.

### Implementation latitude

Codex may choose local Rust types, helper placement, test factoring, and exact error wording provided
that:

- the public spellings and success conditions above hold;
- one canonical product-version owner and one canonical public projection remain;
- internal binding is not weakened;
- generated outputs remain executable-owned;
- the public-surface audit has a maintainable typed/path boundary;
- unrelated internal contract bumps are avoided; and
- all predecessor public paths are deleted.

When local evidence shows that one specified field is not actually public, retain it only at its
internal owner and document the evidence. Do not use that latitude to preserve a user-visible
numbered subsystem surface.

### Non-goals

Do not:

- redesign semantic authority, storage, compilation, runtime evaluation, package management, or the
  type system;
- change artifact or stored-object bytes merely to remove a name from prose;
- synchronize or reset internal contract numbers;
- add a universal edition, generation, compatibility, or migration selector;
- add source syntax, JIT, AOT, SIMD, custom allocation, daemon/session machinery, or broad caching;
- add a new release target, installer, signing system, package registry, or general CI;
- publish `0.1.10`, dispatch a workflow, tag, deploy, or migrate production data;
- rewrite completed campaigns, decisions, evidence, or immutable release assets; or
- perform a blind global terminology replacement that damages exact historical or internal
  evidence.

## 7. Target behavior and ownership boundaries

### 7.1 Product version query

The process dispatcher owns exact `--version` recognition before normal operation dispatch. It uses
`PRODUCT_VERSION` directly and writes one bounded line. It performs no project discovery, repository
read, generated-registry construction, network access, filesystem mutation, or runtime setup.

Unknown or mixed forms use the existing deterministic compact usage failure. Do not introduce a
second help/parser stack solely for this flag.

### 7.2 Capabilities projection

Keep the internal registry snapshot and descriptors available to internal Rust owners. Introduce or
reuse one public projection that:

- begins with a product record containing exactly `name=lkjscript` and the root product version;
- emits a capabilities record containing the opaque digest and any stable cache status;
- emits public operations, grammar, limits, diagnostics, effects, project requirements, and
  nonclaims;
- supports focused command/section discovery and bounded explicit output;
- uses `--known-capabilities` for exact unchanged detection; and
- omits contract tables, internal identity strings, internal version fields, and generation names.

The opaque capabilities digest must be derived from the exact canonical public projection or from a
stronger executable snapshot whose relation to that projection is proved. It must change whenever a
consumer-relevant public capability changes and must not depend on wall clock, path, hash iteration,
or handwritten prose.

Do not expose a private/debug flag on the distributed executable to recover the internal contract
table. Contributor tooling can use typed library APIs or internal test projections.

### 7.3 Logical-plan review projection

The plan token and internal logical-plan encoding continue to bind exact request and prepared
semantic effects. The optional human/machine review file is a derived projection and must stop
rendering internal contract/version fields.

Retain exact authority, base/result revision, semantic state, request commitment digest, effects,
budgets, selected tests, and token needed for review. Where provenance is useful, report the
`lkjscript` product version and capabilities digest. The file is never imported as authority.

Predecessor review-file content is not a current input, so do not add a reader or converter.

### 7.4 Deployment descriptors

The current descriptor shape is strict and unversioned at the product surface. Remove visible
version-discriminator fields while retaining internal typed current-format ownership.

The decoder must reject removed fields before opening the referenced artifact or reading secrets.
Project creation emits only the new shape. Maintained descriptors and generated schema must be
canonical, deterministic, and exact.

Do not replace the removed integers with renamed generation strings, editions, schema names, magic
text, or a product-version field. A descriptor is operator policy for one installed executable; the
executable's product version is discovered separately.

### 7.5 Resident events

Ready, stopped, and failure events are bounded operational observations. Preserve event kind,
success/failure classification, exact artifact/deployment observations, listener address where
applicable, receipt, diagnostics, and cleanup evidence. Remove subsystem version fields and do not
replace them with another protocol number.

### 7.6 Release metadata

Public product metadata binds one product snapshot and may use the product version. It must remain
strict, deterministic, and independently verifiable.

Internal release tooling may carry extra typed state between trusted first-party stages. Separate
that private/transient state from the canonical metadata shipped in the archive. Do not weaken the
no-checkout verifier, candidate digest binding, static inspection, transferred application oracles,
checksum validation, or publication isolation.

When historical public metadata must remain verifiable, bind any old parser to the exact immutable
release identity and keep it read-only. New preparation must have one current writer.

### 7.7 Documentation and terminology

Create or update one normative product-surface owner under `docs/spec/` that defines:

- the product version authority;
- product-facing versus internal/historical/evidence boundaries;
- allowed public identity classes;
- stable unnumbered subsystem vocabulary;
- direct predecessor rejection; and
- generated/current documentation ownership.

Current prose describes behavior, not internal generation ladders. Exact current internal identities
remain discoverable from source owners and structured internal evidence when needed by maintainers.
