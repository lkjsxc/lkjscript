# v0.1.87: owner-local structured worker dispatch

The shared runtime separates physical worker custody from receipt-joined
availability. Reusing a locally available worker and returning its joined receipt
now take only that executor's state lock. They do not acquire the process-wide
custody directory. New physical starts, cross-owner handoff and dispatch closure
retain their shared coordination and exact join ownership.

The bounded directory records one weak owner/slot entry per physical worker while
its executor is open. Active jobs, unreceived results and failed cleanup never
become available merely because their custody is recorded. Local misses drop the
owner lock before entering the shared directory and recheck local availability
and closure afterward. Refused submission preserves the intact unexecuted payload;
failed result disposal retains diagnostics, returns accounting and prevents donation.

A controlled regression preserves the actual predecessor's failure under a held
shared catalogue. Successor obligations cover local and independent-owner progress
under the same obstruction, owner-returning refusal, unwinding cleanup, unreceived
results, bounded concurrent handoff/closure and final reclamation. See the
[decision](../decisions/owner-local-worker-dispatch.md) for invariants and costs and
[status](../status.md) for actual completed acceptance and availability.

This removes two shared-lock acquisitions from a stable local dispatched pair; it
does not claim a measured speedup, lock-free scheduling, fairness, preemption or a
total root-plus-child CPU bound. Cold or contended misses may inspect active owners
and still serialize with shared management. CLI observation 43, shared-runtime
observation 3 and all semantic, artifact and application-data encodings are unchanged.
No migration, new grant, channel, active-task movement or cross-process sharing is
introduced. Product numbering remains opaque; publication requires its own unchanged
final-archive acceptance and ordinary release procedure.
